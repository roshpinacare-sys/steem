# V33 Evidence-Backed Roadmap to Category Leadership

**Program:** Evidence-Carrying Autonomy Platform  
**Current baseline:** v32.18 Integrated Pilot, production blocked  
**North-star business hypothesis:** a category-leading enterprise platform capable of supporting a future $300M–$500M valuation  
**Planning discipline:** valuation is an outcome of customer value, revenue quality, defensibility, and trust—not a code-volume target.

## North-star outcome

Create the independent authority and assurance layer that allows enterprises to move from AI that recommends work to AI that performs real work. The system must remain provider-neutral, sovereign-deployable, easy to operate through chat, low-interruption, independently verifiable, and recoverable.

## Value proof required for a $300M–$500M outcome

The roadmap treats the valuation target as a design pressure test. A defensible outcome likely requires one of the following profiles:

- approximately $40M–$60M ARR with category leadership and strong growth;
- a lower ARR level only if strategic defensibility, growth and platform control are exceptional;
- enterprise ACV that supports a focused direct-sales motion rather than a services-heavy custom-project business;
- high gross margin, strong retention and demonstrable expansion.

These are target conditions, not a valuation promise.

## Phase 1 — Category and Buyer Validation

**Purpose:** Prove that the problem is urgent, funded, understandable, and not merely a platform feature bundled by incumbents.

### Product statement under test

> We let enterprise AI agents perform real engineering changes under explicit authority, independent evidence, exact recovery, and provider-neutral governance.

### Minimum evidence

- 15 structured interviews with CTO, VP Engineering, Head of Platform, CISO or AppSec leaders.
- 5 participants classify the problem as urgent and budget-relevant.
- 3 qualified design partners agree to provide a real repository and workflow.
- 2 paid pilots or paid discovery engagements.
- Recorded objections and a falsifiable competitor map.

### Kill or narrow criteria

- Buyers consistently classify the product as an existing GitHub/Microsoft/Google feature.
- No buyer will provide a real workflow or budget.
- The governance layer adds more human work than it removes.

### Exit artifact

`V33_CATEGORY_VALIDATION_REPORT` with interview evidence, buyer language, pricing evidence, competitor displacement and revised category definition.

## Phase 2 — Chat-Native Operator and Push Approval

**Purpose:** Make the system usable by a person who should not operate infrastructure screens.

### Vertical UX

`Chat request → mission interpretation → bounded plan → autonomous preparation → one concise push decision → execution/result → evidence summary`

### Required capabilities

- Persistent mission chat.
- Minimum-question intent clarification.
- Policy-derived approval requirements.
- Mobile/web push cards: Yes, No, Change conditions, Explain.
- Simple and expert evidence views.
- Approval expiry, delegation and emergency stop.
- User decisions recorded as signed evidence.

### Proof gate

- At least 70% reduction in user interaction steps versus the current manual process.
- Median ordinary workflow requires no more than one approval.
- Approval decision is correctly understood by test users in under ten seconds.
- No authority expansion through learned preference.
- Accessibility and destructive-action recovery tests pass.

## Phase 3 — Governed Agentic Engineering Vertical Slice

**Purpose:** Complete one commercially valuable workflow end to end.

### Target workflow

`“Fix the defect introduced in the latest change.”`

The platform must:

1. identify the allowed repository and authority;
2. inspect and plan across multiple files;
3. execute in an independently isolated workspace;
4. write or strengthen tests;
5. run build, test, security and change-impact gates;
6. create an Action Passport and rollback package;
7. request a single push approval when policy requires it;
8. create a real pull request under bounded credentials;
9. observe CI and report the result;
10. never merge or deploy beyond policy.

### Proof gate

- Three representative real repositories.
- At least 30 bounded change missions.
- Zero unauthorized external effects.
- 100% effect receipts and evidence-chain closure.
- 100% tested rollback or compensating path for reversible missions.
- Independent reviewer separation demonstrated.
- Measurable reduction in engineer and reviewer time.
- Failure cases include malicious repository content, prompt injection, dependency compromise, stolen approval, replay, tenant crossover and provider outage.

### Commercial milestone

A repeatable paid-pilot package with defined installation, support boundary, pricing hypothesis and success criteria.

## Phase 4 — External Trust and Sovereign Runtime Qualification

**Purpose:** Replace local self-evidence with independently observed operational proof.

### Qualification tracks

- External KMS/HSM custody and rotation.
- Workload identity, federation and revocation.
- PostgreSQL HA, RLS, backup, PITR and restore.
- Multi-host durable execution and effect deduplication.
- Independently observed telemetry and SLOs.
- Container, WASM or microVM isolation for untrusted workloads.
- Penetration test, supply-chain and license review.
- Recovery game days and compromised-key drills.

### Proof gate

- Independent organizations own collector, reviewer and witness roles.
- Zero unauthorized effects in adversarial qualification.
- Recovery and revocation meet declared SLOs.
- No self-contained trust bundle can qualify itself.
- Air-gapped or private deployment path is reproducible.

## Phase 5 — Proof-Carrying Adaptive Execution

**Purpose:** Reduce model cost, latency, user approvals and operational friction without reducing assurance.

### SRS-derived bounded workflow

`Full governed histories → shortcut candidate → independent holdout → mutation/fuzz/differential replay → shadow execution → Shortcut Passport → user policy approval → limited automation → drift revocation`

### Proof gate

- At least one recurrent workflow is safely compressed.
- Shadow result agreement meets a domain-specific threshold with zero unauthorized divergence.
- Tool, model, policy or environment drift invalidates the shortcut.
- Fallback to the full workflow always remains available.
- Demonstrated reduction in user approvals, model calls, cost or latency.
- Research lab has no production credentials and cannot promote its own candidate.

# Program-level decision rule

A cycle is complete only when it improves all three dimensions:

1. **User value:** less work, faster outcome or better decision.
2. **Trust:** stronger authority, evidence, isolation, verification or recovery.
3. **Commercial proof:** improved willingness to pay, repeatability, margin or defensibility.

A capability that improves none of these is not roadmap progress.
