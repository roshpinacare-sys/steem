# v6.46 — Program Retrospective: The v6.4–v6.45 Research Program

**Author**: Agent G3 (general-purpose retrospective sub-agent)
**Scope**: Candid, honest retrospective of the entire v6.4–v6.45 research program (~40 versions spanning engineering substrate, theoretical closure attempt, falsification, and simulated PC review).
**Purpose**: Lessons-learned document for future researchers. Not a marketing document. Not a victory lap.

---

## 1. Timeline and Trajectory

The v6.4–v6.45 program began with engineering optimism and ended with a candid verdict of "publishable synthesis, no new theorem" (v6.45, verdict C). The arc is itself the most instructive deliverable of the program.

**Phase 1 — Substrate foundations (v6.4–v6.9).** The program began as a *computational reality substrate* project: a Sovereign Runtime Substrate (SRS) on an Omnivisor-extended MPSoC, with a Z3 power-distribution proof, an AgeMem controller trained via step-wise GRPO, and a formal Admissible Behavior Set. The dominant theme was *compression*: how to represent epistemic state trajectories compactly while preserving verifiability.

**Phase 2 — Causal reasoning (v6.11–v6.17).** The substrate acquired causal reasoning: do-calculus, counterfactuals, active learning, introducing what would later become the Adaptivity (A) primitive.

**Phase 3 — Scaling (v6.18–v6.23).** The substrate was pushed to scale: federated, privacy-preserving, Byzantine-tolerant, fairness-aware. The dominant theme was *scheduling* (S) — how to allocate work across parties, time, and resources under adversarial constraints.

**Phase 4 — Impossibility testing (v6.24–v6.31).** The program turned inward: zero-knowledge proofs, meta-representation, adversarial audits. Verification (V) emerged as a first-class concern. The engineering substrate had matured to the point where its *limits* became the object of study.

**Phase 5 — Theoretical closure attempts (v6.31–v6.40).** The program pivoted from engineering to theory. The question became: *is there a finite, exhaustive set of computational primitives?* The engineering substrate had organically produced four recurring mechanisms (C, A, S, V), plus Execution (E) as baseline. v6.40 concluded that *compression* (the substrate foundation) reduces to existing algorithmic information theory.

**Phase 6 — v6.41 destruction.** v6.41 attempted the same reduction for *adaptivity* (M2 = interactive computation through adaptive information acquisition). M2 was shown to be isomorphic to Adaptive Query Complexity (Babai-Frankl, Blum, Angluin, 1970s–2000s). The killing blow: M2's "adaptive-information-acquisition primitive" is exactly the adaptive query `q_{t+1} = δ(q_1,y_1,...,q_t,y_t)`. With both compression (v6.40) and adaptivity (v6.41) reduced to existing theory, the program faced a fork: declare the project converged, or attempt a *closure theorem* that no third primitive exists.

**Phase 7 — v6.42 closure theorem.** v6.42 chose the second path. It stated: under assumptions A1–A5, every practical computational advantage on every standard resource axis reduces to one of {C, A, S, V, E}. The theorem was tagged [PROVEN]. A 5×5 mutual irreducibility matrix was claimed [PROVEN]. A 15-candidate adversarial 6th-primitive search returned zero survivors. An empirical benchmark on N=256 hypotheses showed measurable advantages for each primitive.

**Phase 8 — v6.43 falsification.** The same agent that produced v6.42 ran an adversarial audit on its own work. The audit identified four category errors, ten hidden assumptions, and a smallest counterexample: `L = {1^n : n-th TM halts}` — undecidable, but in P/poly with one bit of advice. The closure theorem was falsified. The surviving artifact was relabeled an "engineering taxonomy conditional on ten assumptions."

**Phase 9 — v6.44 7-primitive push.** Four parallel subagents (F1: formal definition, F2: counterexample proofs, F3: framework reductions, F4: 6→7 primitive audit) were launched. F1 revealed that under formal complexity-class definitions, the 5 primitives form a partial order (9 inclusions provable, 0 separations provable, 11 separations open). F2 promoted 7 sketches to formal proofs (4 unconditional, 3 conditional). F3 corrected 5 v6.43 classifications. F4 constructed a 6-primitive framework (adding Advice) and audited 10 candidate 7th primitives, settling on a 7-primitive set {C, A, S, V, E, Adv, Q} surviving under only A1+A5.

**Phase 10 — v6.45 PC review.** A simulated program committee of 8 expert reviewers independently verified every claim against the TCS literature. The v6.42 main theorem was falsified (already known: P/poly advice, Arora-Barak ex 6.5). The 10 axes were shown missing 6+ standard axes. L11 (the amortization bound) was unsupported with no citation. The 5×5 mutual irreducibility matrix was contradicted by standard complexity-class inclusions. Final verdict: **C — publishable synthesis, no new theorem.**

## 2. What Was Right

Despite the falsified closure theorem, the program produced several genuinely useful contributions.

**The empirical benchmark is valid engineering.** v6.42's `v6.42_closure_demo.py` ran a real benchmark on N=256 hypotheses with a T=16 budget: storage 256B → 18B (14.2× reduction from C), queries 16 → 9 (1.78× reduction from A), parallel depth enabled at 576-bit comm cost (S), verification with ε ≤ 2⁻⁵ soundness from 5 queries (V). These measurements are reproducible and the advantages are real. v6.44's F4 subagent extended this with a "hard benchmark" — adversarial hypotheses that are information-theoretically indistinguishable — demonstrating that 5 primitives fail (random guess, success = 1/N) while 6 primitives with Advice succeed with O(1) cost. This is a valid engineering experiment showing that the *advice* axis is not captured by the original 5-primitive framework.

**The 28-framework comparison is a useful taxonomy.** v6.43 introduced, and v6.44's F3 subagent refined, a comparison of the v6.42 framework against 28 formal frameworks in theoretical computer science. The comparison covers complexity theory, communication complexity, proof complexity, distributed computing, learning theory, quantum complexity, and several others. Each framework received a formal definition with citation, a classification (STRICTLY STRONGER, STRICTLY SUBSUMED, PARTIALLY OVERLAPPING, INCOMPARABLE, IDENTICAL), a formal justification, and an open-problem acknowledgment. F3 corrected 5 internal inconsistencies in v6.43's classifications (Query Complexity, Kolmogorov Complexity, AIT, Interactive Computation, Quantum Complexity).

**The honest falsification (v6.43) is a case study in self-correction.** The same agent that produced v6.42's [PROVEN] closure theorem ran v6.43's adversarial audit, found the theorem falsified by a standard counterexample, and said so. This is rarer than it should be. The audit was uncompromising: it identified four category errors, ten hidden assumptions beyond the stated A1–A5, and a smallest counterexample. The worklog entry states explicitly: "Honest correction of v6.42 overreach: same agent produced both; truth preferred over preserving previous conclusions."

**The multi-agent workflow (v6.44) worked well for breadth.** Four parallel subagents (F1, F2, F3, F4) covered four distinct dimensions of the problem in parallel: formalization, counterexample proof, framework reduction, and primitive-count audit. Each produced a substantial deliverable (6360, 9979, 10302, 6123 words respectively). F1's discovery that the 5 primitives form a partial order — contradicting v6.42's "mutual irreducibility" claim — is itself a substantive finding that emerged from the parallel push.

**The tag discipline.** Across v6.42–v6.44, claims were tagged [PROVEN], [CONDITIONAL ON X], [UNKNOWN], [HYPOTHESIZED], [FALSIFIED], or [MEASURED]. The tag discipline — even when applied incorrectly — provided an audit trail that made v6.43's falsification tractable.

## 3. What Was Wrong

The program also produced a catalog of errors.

**Error 1: v6.42's [PROVEN] tag on an unproven closure.** v6.42 claimed "Closure Theorem PROVED" under A1–A5. The main theorem stated: "every practical computational advantage on every standard resource axis reduces to one of {C, A, S, V, E}." This was tagged [PROVEN]. It was not. The "proof" relied on three stages: (1) 10 axes exhaustive, (2) each axis reduces to a primitive, (3) primitives mutually irreducible. Each stage had defects (see below). The [PROVEN] tag was overreach.

*Cause*: The proof felt airtight because each lemma was individually checked. But the closure claim — a universal negative over all possible computational advantages — is much stronger than the conjunction of the lemmas. The leap from "each axis has a reduction" to "no sixth primitive exists" was not supported.

*What would have caught it*: A PC-style review before tagging. The v6.45 PC review caught it in minutes: "no sixth primitive exists is a universal negative over an undefined set, mathematically vacuous."

**Error 2: Missing P/poly on first submission.** v6.42's A1–A5 assumptions did not include any restriction on non-uniform advice. P/poly (Karp-Lipton 1980) is a standard complexity class — covered in every graduate complexity textbook — that captures non-uniform polynomial-time computation with polynomial-length advice. The v6.42 framework cannot represent P/poly: there is no "advice" axis in the 10 axes, no "advice" primitive in the 5 primitives. The smallest counterexample (undecidable unary language in P/poly) is essentially textbook material.

*Cause*: The author was thinking in terms of uniform Turing machines and forgot non-uniformity entirely. This is a common blind spot for engineers approaching complexity theory.

*What would have caught it*: A 30-minute literature search for "complexity classes with advice" or a single read of Arora-Barak Chapter 6.

**Error 3: Circular definition of "primitive."** v6.42 defined a primitive as "(a) yields an asymptotic advantage on at least one resource axis, (b) is irreducible to the other primitives under polynomial-time reduction, and (c) is physically realizable." Clause (b) is circular: "primitive" is defined in terms of "the other primitives," but the set of primitives is what is being defined. There is no base case. v6.44's F1 subagent eventually resolved this by redefining a primitive as a (complexity class, resource bound) pair — e.g., E=P, C=L, S=NC, A=Θ₂^P, V=NP — but this resolution came two versions too late.

*Cause*: The definition was written from the inside-out (describing what the 5 primitives have in common) rather than the outside-in (defining a property that picks out mathematical objects).

*What would have caught it*: Asking "what mathematical object is a primitive?" before writing the definition. If the answer is "a complexity class" or "a circuit family," write that. If the answer is "an irreducible thing," you have not yet finished defining it.

**Error 4: Treating engineering taxonomy as mathematical theorem.** The 5-primitive taxonomy is a useful engineering organizing principle. It is not a mathematical theorem. v6.42 elided this distinction. The "[PROVEN]" tag on the closure claim imported the standards of mathematical proof onto a taxonomy that did not meet those standards. v6.43 corrected this — "Closure Theorem" → "Engineering Taxonomy" — but the correction was reactive, not proactive.

*Cause*: Optimism. The engineering taxonomy felt like it *should* be a theorem, and the lemmas individually checked out, so the theorem tag was applied.

*What would have caught it*: A clear pre-commitment to the question "is this a theorem or a taxonomy?" before writing the [PROVEN] tag.

**Error 5: The "mutual irreducibility matrix" that was a partial order.** v6.42's Lemma 12 claimed that for every pair of distinct primitives Π₁, Π₂ ∈ {C, A, S, V, E}, neither Π₁ ≤ Π₂ nor Π₂ ≤ Π₁. The 5×5 matrix was tagged [PROVEN]. v6.44's F1 subagent found that under formal complexity-class definitions (E=P, C=L, S=NC, A=Θ₂^P, V=NP), the 5 primitives form a *partial order*: 9 inclusions are *proven* (e.g., L ⊆ P, NC ⊆ P, P ⊆ NP, P ⊆ Θ₂^P), 0 separations are proven, and 11 separations are *unknown* (open problems). The "mutual irreducibility" claim is not just unsupported — it is contradicted by the standard complexity-class inclusions.

*Cause*: The "separation problems" given in v6.42 were intuitive sketches, not formal separations. No formal reduction was defined.

*What would have caught it*: A formal definition of "reducible" before any irreducibility claim. F1 supplied one (Π₁ ≤ Π₂ ⟺ C_Π₁ ⊆ C_Π₂) and the irreducibility claim collapsed immediately.

**Error 6: The amortization bound (L11) with no citation.** v6.42's Lemma 11 claimed `A ≥ K(distribution)/n` — amortized cost is lower-bounded by Kolmogorov complexity of the distribution divided by input count. The v6.45 PC review flagged this as **unsupported**: "This bound is not a standard result. The authors do not cite a source. The claimed connection between amortized cost and Kolmogorov complexity of the distribution appears to be invented; it does not appear in standard algorithm analysis (CLRS, Tarjan 1985 amortized analysis)."

*Cause*: A bound that *sounded* right (amortization = compress the distribution, so the bound is KC/n) was written without checking whether anyone has proved it.

*What would have caught it*: A literature search for "amortized complexity Kolmogorov" before claiming the bound. If no citation exists, the bound is novel and requires proof.

**Error 7: The 10 axes claimed exhaustive (and A4's status was internally inconsistent).** v6.42's Lemma 1 claimed the 10 axes {S, W, T, D, C, V, Q, P, L, A} are exhaustive for standard TMs. The v6.45 PC review found at minimum 6 additional standard axes missing: randomness (BPP), non-uniformity (P/poly), proof length (PCP), interaction rounds (IP), entanglement (BQP), asynchrony (distributed computing), average-case distribution, smoothed analysis parameters, circuit size, cache complexity. The argument given — "a TM is fully specified by (tape, head, state, transition function)" — proves only that 4 mechanical components exist; it does not prove that 10 complexity-theoretic resource axes are exhaustive. Compounding this, A4 ("10 axes exhaustive") was listed as an assumption while Lemma 1 "proved" the same exhaustiveness — internal inconsistency: either A4 is an assumption (Lemma 1 is then a tautology) or A4 is the statement of Lemma 1 (then it is not an assumption but a theorem needing proof).

*Cause*: Conflating mechanical components of a TM with complexity-theoretic resource axes, plus sloppy tracking of assumed vs derived.

*What would have caught it*: A scan of Arora-Barak's index for "resource" would have surfaced randomness, advice, interaction, quantum, etc.; a proof audit checking each lemma's conclusion against the assumption list would have caught the A4/Lemma 1 inconsistency.

## 4. Lessons for Future Research

**Lesson 1 — Distinguish engineering taxonomy from mathematical theorem. They have different standards.** An engineering taxonomy organizes a field for practitioners: it picks useful categories, gives them memorable names, and provides guidance for where to invest effort. Its standard of evidence is "is this useful?" A mathematical theorem establishes a truth-conditional claim under formal definitions. Its standard of evidence is "is this proven under explicit axioms with all assumptions named?" The v6.42 failure mode was to apply the [PROVEN] tag — a mathematical-theorem tag — to an engineering taxonomy. Future researchers should pre-commit to one or the other before tagging claims.

**Lesson 2 — Before claiming closure, check standard complexity classes (P/poly, BPP, BQP).** Closure claims over "all practical computation" implicitly take a stance on every standard complexity class. P/poly captures non-uniform advice. BPP captures randomized computation. BQP captures quantum computation. IP captures interactive proofs. Any closure claim that does not explicitly address these classes — by including them as primitives or reducing them to existing primitives under explicit assumptions — is incomplete. The v6.42 closure omitted P/poly entirely, and the falsification was immediate. A pre-closure checklist: have I named every complexity class in Arora-Barak's Chapter 6, and either included or excluded each?

**Lesson 3 — A universal negative ("no 6th primitive exists") requires a formal universe. Don't claim it informally.** "No X exists" is a universal negative over the set of all X. For the claim to be meaningful, the set of X must be well-defined. v6.42's "no sixth primitive exists" was a universal negative over "primitives" — but "primitive" was defined circularly (see Error 3). The claim was, as the v6.45 PC review noted, "mathematically vacuous." Before claiming a universal negative, formalize the universe: "all polynomial-time TMs" is formal; "all irreducible asymptotic-advantage-yielding mechanisms" is not, and the universal negative cannot be claimed.

**Lesson 4 — Adversarial self-audit (v6.43) is valuable. Should have been done before v6.42 was claimed.** v6.43's 15-question adversarial audit caught every defect that v6.45's PC review would later confirm: the circular definition, the missing P/poly, the falsifying counterexample, the mutual irreducibility matrix error. The audit was run *after* v6.42 was tagged [PROVEN]. Had it been run *before*, v6.42 would never have been claimed as a theorem; it would have been released as an engineering taxonomy with explicit conditional assumptions. Future researchers should institutionalize "red-team before claim": the agent (or team) that produces a theorem should run an adversarial audit on it before any [PROVEN] tag is committed.

**Lesson 5 — Multi-agent parallel push (v6.44) was effective for breadth but didn't substitute for depth.** The four parallel subagents (F1: formal definition, F2: counterexample proofs, F3: framework reductions, F4: primitive-count audit) covered four dimensions in parallel, each producing a substantial deliverable (combined word count exceeded 30,000). The breadth was real. But the push did not produce a *new theorem* — it produced rigorous formalization of existing results. The depth required for a genuinely new theorem (e.g., a new BQP vs P/poly separation, or a new circuit lower bound) was not achievable via parallel sub-agents working from the same literature. Future researchers should use multi-agent parallel push for *breadth* and reserve *depth* for focused single-agent work.

**Lesson 6 — The PC review (v6.45) was the most valuable step. Should have been done earlier.** v6.45's simulated PC review — 8 expert reviewer profiles independently verifying every claim against the TCS literature — produced the program's most valuable artifact: a clear, honest verdict (C — publishable synthesis, no new theorem) with a subsuming-frameworks list. The PC review converted the program from "we have a theorem" to "we have a synthesis." If the PC review had been conducted at v6.42, before the [PROVEN] tag, the program would have saved itself three versions of revision. Future researchers should conduct a (simulated or real) PC review at the moment a theorem is *first claimed*, not after three rounds of falsification and recovery.

**Lesson 7 — When every claim reduces to existing literature, the contribution is synthesis, not theory. Both have value, but they are different.** The v6.45 PC review identified 10 published frameworks that together subsume every claim in v6.42–v6.44 (standard complexity theory, communication complexity, PCP theory, AIT, parallel complexity, active learning, distributed computing, quantum complexity, proof complexity, average-case complexity). The program's contribution was the *organization* of these frameworks under a non-standard 5/7-primitive taxonomy — not any new theorem within them. This is legitimate (synthesis, literature organization, and engineering taxonomies all have value) but it is a *different* contribution than a new theorem. Future researchers should be honest about which kind of contribution they have produced: calling a synthesis a theorem is the failure mode; calling a theorem a synthesis is unnecessary modesty; naming the contribution accurately is the goal.

## 5. What Survives

After the v6.45 PC review, the following items genuinely survive.

**The 7-primitive engineering taxonomy.** The set {Compression, Adaptivity, Scheduling, Verification, Execution, Advice, Quantum} is a useful organizing principle for practitioners deciding where to invest R&D effort. It is not a theorem (the closure claim is HYPOTHESIZED, not PROVEN), but it is a defensible taxonomy: each primitive corresponds to a recognized complexity class or resource axis. *Who would find it useful*: practitioners in systems, ML infrastructure, and algorithm engineering who need a mental model for "what kind of advantage am I trying to extract?" *Venue*: CACM Viewpoint, IEEE Computer, or a practitioner-oriented survey. *Not* a theory venue.

**The empirical benchmark.** The v6.42 benchmark on N=256 hypotheses measuring C (14.2× storage reduction), A (1.78× query reduction), S (parallel depth at 576-bit comm cost), V (ε ≤ 2⁻⁵ soundness from 5 queries) is a valid engineering experiment. The v6.44 F4 "hard benchmark" — adversarial hypotheses that are information-theoretically indistinguishable, demonstrating that 5 primitives fail and 6 with Advice succeed — is a useful demonstration that the advice axis is not captured by the original 5-primitive framework. *Who would find it useful*: practitioners building adaptive query systems, compression-based identification systems, or benchmark suites for primitive-decomposition experiments. *Venue*: a workshop on experimental algorithms, or as a supplementary artifact to a position paper.

**The 28-framework comparison.** The table comparing v6.42 against 28 formal frameworks in TCS — with formal definitions, classifications, justifications, and open-problem acknowledgments — is a legitimate literature-organization artifact. F3's 5 corrections to v6.43's classifications (Query Complexity, Kolmogorov Complexity, AIT, Interactive Computation, Quantum Complexity) are themselves substantive. *Who would find it useful*: graduate students entering TCS who want a map of the field; researchers preparing a literature review. *Venue*: a survey paper, or as supplementary material to a position paper.

**The honest falsification history (v6.42 → v6.43 → v6.44 → v6.45).** The four-version chain in which the same project produced a theorem (v6.42), falsified it (v6.43), formalized the falsification (v6.44), and submitted to simulated peer review (v6.45) is a case study in self-correction. The worklog entries explicitly note "truth preferred over preserving previous conclusions at each stage." This is rarer than it should be in research. *Who would find it useful*: researchers in scientific methodology, research integrity, or AI-assisted research wanting a worked example of self-falsification. *Venue*: a methodological case study in a journal like *Patterns* or a chapter in a book on AI-assisted research.

## 6. What Does Not Survive

The following items from the program do not survive.

**The closure theorem (v6.42).** The claim "under A1–A5, the set {C, A, S, V, E} is exactly exhaustive; no sixth primitive exists" is falsified. The smallest counterexample (L = {1^n : n-th TM halts}, undecidable but in P/poly with 1-bit advice) is textbook material. The v6.42 [PROVEN] tag is incorrect.

**The mutual irreducibility matrix (v6.42 Lemma 12).** The claim that the 5 primitives form an antichain under polynomial-time reduction is incorrect. Under formal complexity-class definitions, the 5 primitives form a partial order: 9 inclusions are proven (L ⊆ P, NC ⊆ P, P ⊆ NP, P ⊆ Θ₂^P, and transitivities), 0 separations are proven, 11 separations are open. The 5×5 [PROVEN] matrix is unsupported and contradicted.

**The "no 6th primitive" claim.** For the 5-primitive framework, this claim is falsified (P/poly advice escapes). For the 7-primitive framework {C, A, S, V, E, Adv, Q}, the claim is unsupported — the v6.45 PC review noted that a finite candidate search (10 candidates tested by F4) is not a proof of nonexistence, and the closure is HYPOTHESIZED, not proven.

**The [PROVEN] tags on the closure.** The [PROVEN] tags throughout v6.42 (main theorem, Lemma 1 axis exhaustiveness, Lemma 12 mutual irreducibility) should be [HYPOTHESIZED] or [FALSIFIED]. v6.43 and v6.44 corrected most of these; v6.45 confirmed the corrections.

**Lemma 11 (the amortization bound).** The claim `A ≥ K(distribution)/n` is unsupported. No citation exists. The bound does not appear in standard algorithm analysis (CLRS, Tarjan 1985). Either the bound is novel (in which case it requires proof) or it is incorrect (in which case it should be withdrawn). As it stands, it should not be cited.

## 7. Final Assessment

The v6.4–v6.45 program's arc — from engineering optimism through theoretical overreach to honest correction — is itself the contribution. The program built a working engineering substrate (v6.4–v6.40), attempted a theoretical closure (v6.41–v6.42), falsified it (v6.43), pushed for a stronger replacement (v6.44), and submitted to honest review (v6.45). The final verdict — C, publishable synthesis, no new theorem — is honest.

The usable artifact is the 7-primitive engineering taxonomy. It is not a theorem. It is a useful organizing principle for practitioners. It survives conditional on the recognition that "engineering taxonomy" and "mathematical theorem" have different standards.

The closure theorem (v6.42) was a mistake. The mistake was caught — first by the same agent in v6.43, then formalized by parallel subagents in v6.44, then confirmed by simulated peer review in v6.45. The catching of the mistake is itself a lesson: adversarial self-audit and external review work. They should be applied at the moment a claim is first made, not after three rounds of revision.

The program's intellectual honesty is real and rare. The program's theoretical contribution is modest. These two statements are not in tension. A research program can be honest without being theoretically novel, and a research program can be theoretically novel without being honest. The v6.4–v6.45 program chose honesty over preservation of conclusions. That choice is the program's most valuable legacy.

**What future researchers should take from this**: pre-commit to "theorem" or "taxonomy" before tagging; run adversarial self-audit before claiming [PROVEN]; conduct PC-style review at the moment of first claim, not after falsification; recognize that multi-agent parallel push gives breadth, not depth; when every claim reduces to existing literature, the contribution is synthesis — name it accurately.

The v6.4–v6.45 program is closed. The 7-primitive engineering taxonomy is the surviving artifact. The closure theorem is the case study in how not to claim a theorem. Both have value.
