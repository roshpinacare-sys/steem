## 4. Does the Adversarial Benchmark Now Honestly Demonstrate the 5-prim vs 6-prim Separation?

**No — and that is the honest answer.**

The v7.2 benchmark runs the REAL `adaptivity.execute_identify` (the actual 5-prim algorithm) on the adversarial hypotheses and reports that it **SUCCEEDS with 6 queries**. The "5-prim FAILS, 6-prim SUCCEEDS" separation claimed in v7.1 was **manufactured** by swapping in a deliberately broken strawman (`_adversarial_5prim`) that:

1. Used `adversarial_hash_bit` (a fake function no real primitive calls)
2. Passed a fake `[0]*k` prefix instead of the real prefix
3. Queried `x = 0, 1, 2, ...` (all < k) instead of sampling randomly

Under the REAL `hash_bit` (SHA-256-based), the adversarial hypotheses are **perfectly distinguishable from x=0 onwards**. The v7.2 benchmark also tried building genuinely-indistinguishable hypotheses (via birthday-style search on real `hash_bit`), but even those are distinguishable by real adaptivity because adaptivity samples `x` from `[0, 4096)` and queries `x >= k` where the hyps differ.

**Conclusion:** The 5-prim vs 6-prim separation is a **theoretical result** that requires a controlled query function (like `adversarial_hash_bit`), not the real SHA-256-based `hash_bit`. The v7.2 benchmark honestly reports this and no longer claims the separation can be demonstrated empirically on the real runtime.

The honest verdict (printed by the benchmark):
> "HONEST VERDICT (R4.1/R4.2 fix): the REAL 5-prim (adaptivity.execute_identify) SUCCEEDS on the 'adversarial' hypotheses with 6 queries. The adversarial prefix only applies under `adversarial_hash_bit` (a fake function no real primitive calls); under the real `hash_bit`, the hypotheses are perfectly distinguishable from x=0 onwards. The v7.1 '5-prim FAILS, 6-prim SUCCEEDS' separation was manufactured by swapping in a deliberately broken strawman. Under honest measurement, the 5-prim vs 6-prim separation CANNOT be demonstrated on these hypotheses."
